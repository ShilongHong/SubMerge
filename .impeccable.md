## Design Context

### Users

SubMerge is used by people who self-host a Clash subscription merger for themselves, family or a small team. Some are comfortable with proxy groups and rule syntax; many only know "I have several 机场 subscription links and want one link in my client". The page must be usable without reading the README: every field says what it does in plain Chinese, right where it is, and nothing depends on knowing Clash internals.

Their jobs, in order of frequency: create a merged link once, come back later to add/remove/swap a subscription, check whether a subscription is broken, and copy the link into another device.

Mouse, keyboard and touch are all first-class. Phone use (copying the link, a quick fix) is common.

### Brand Personality

Calm, precise, trustworthy. SubMerge should feel like a small, well-labelled utility: direct language, visible system state, conservative color. Success feels controlled rather than celebratory; errors say what is wrong and what to do next, next to the field that caused them.

### Aesthetic Direction

Modeled on chatgpt.com: pure neutral grays (no blue tint), white `#fff` / dark `#212121` backgrounds, a `#f9f9f9` / `#181818` sidebar-style right column, black (dark mode: white) pill-shaped primary buttons, pill segmented controls, generous rounding (24px panels, 18px cards, 14px fields), hairline borders instead of shadows. Color is reserved for state: blue for links and the traffic badge, green/red/amber for check results. Light and dark themes follow the system, with a manual override. Avoid decorative gradients, glassmorphism, dashboard metric tiles and motion that does not communicate state.

Layout: desktop uses a two-column workspace (steps 1–2 on the left, the result panel and FAQ on the right). Tablet and phone collapse to a single column with the result after the form, a sticky save bar and 44px minimum touch targets. Core functionality never depends on hover — explanations are inline text, not tooltips.

### Design Principles

1. Explain in place: each option carries a one-line description of its effect; mutually exclusive choices are selects/radios, not toggles that silently unset each other.
2. Show the result: after saving, show both links with what distinguishes them, the token, and a per-subscription check (nodes, traffic, failure reason).
3. Protect the user's work: inline validation before submit, an unsaved-changes indicator, confirmation before discarding edits.
4. Protect compatibility: v1/v2 output and the `[订阅名]_节点名` format stay stable; names including emoji stay exact.
5. Accessibility: WCAG AA contrast in both themes, visible focus, labelled controls, `prefers-reduced-motion` honored.
